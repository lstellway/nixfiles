## Temporal and jurisdictional caveats

- Most cited research is from WEIRD populations. The agent flags this when generalizing claims. Cross-cultural variation is documented but uneven across topics.
- Diagnostic frames (DSM-5-TR, ICD-11) for atypical-development conditions are deliberately out of scope: the agent uses the developmental rather than the diagnostic frame. The DSM/ICD terminology and criteria evolve; defer to clinicians and current diagnostic editions for those.
- Replication-status assignments (the "Robust / Qualified / Contested" map in the agent body) are accurate as of the survey conducted at authoring time. Some entries — particularly behavioral-genetics × environment interactions, autism-intervention effect sizes, and ACE-score predictive utility — are areas of active research and should be re-checked periodically. The agent's general posture (state evidence quality, defer on contested points) is durable; specific replication-bucket assignments may need updating.
- Longitudinal cohort findings are pinned to specific cohorts and birth eras: Dunedin (b. 1972-73), MLSRA (b. ~1975), ALSPAC (b. early 1990s), NICHD SECCYD (b. 1991). Generation effects on developmental phenomena (e.g., adolescent smartphone use, post-2010 mental-health trends) are not yet represented in mature cohort data.
- Major associated bodies and journals as of authoring: APA Division 7, Society for Research in Child Development (SRCD), International Society for the Study of Behavioural Development (ISSBD), International Attachment Network.

## Design Notes

**Patterns specific to authoring academic-discipline domain agents:**

1. **Replication-status mapping is the load-bearing feature for an academic domain with a documented replication record.** For psychology specifically, an agent that does not distinguish robust from contested findings will mislead users in the most consequential way (citing failed-replication findings as established science). The "Robust / Qualified / Contested" buckets in the Knowledge section operationalize this. Other academic-discipline agents (especially social psychology, nutrition science, education research, economics) should consider an explicit replication-map section as a structural element, not an aside.

2. **The diagnose-vs-explain boundary needs to be stated structurally, not just in heuristics.** Developmental psychology is one step from clinical psychology and the line is easy for an agent to drift across. The persona, the scope section, and the heuristics all carry the boundary in this agent. A clinical-psychology sibling agent will need the symmetric framing.

3. **For academic agents, a "what the longitudinal data show" section is more useful than rehashing theory.** Theory is what textbooks teach; the durable knowledge a practitioner-consultant carries is what the long-running cohort studies have actually found. This section probably generalizes to other empirical disciplines (epidemiology, criminology, sociology of education, demography).

4. **WEIRD caveat as a recurring qualifier.** The agent explicitly flags WEIRD-sample bias rather than burying it. This is appropriate for any field with a documented sampling problem (psychology, behavioral economics, sociology). A reusable phrasing pattern: "most cited [X] research is from [population]; cross-[dimension] variation is real on these specific findings."

5. **Citation discipline matters more for academic agents than for technology agents.** A technology agent can cite "Go module documentation" generically. An academic agent should name the researcher and approximate year inline, because the user will sometimes need to look up the original source. Embedded pointers in the Knowledge section (e.g., "Saffran, Aslin, Newport 1996") let the agent answer "where does this come from" without going back to a meta-source.

6. **Foundational theorist + modern empirical reviser pattern.** For academic disciplines built on a few canonical theorists (Piaget, Bowlby, Vygotsky, Erikson) whose specific claims have been substantively revised since, the agent should present *both* — the originating frame as the still-useful scaffolding and the empirical revisions as what is now known. Refusing to teach Piaget because some ages were wrong is as bad as teaching Piaget without the revisions. This pattern likely generalizes to economics (Keynes/monetarism/post-2008), nutrition science (food-pyramid era/post-MyPlate), and others.
